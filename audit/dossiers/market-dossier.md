### The gaps nobody fills (Martlet AI's openings)

1. **Published evaluation methodology.** Nobody explains how their accuracy numbers are produced — no gold-standard definition, no precision/recall split, no per-HCC performance, no drift monitoring. RAAPID's "98% accuracy" and Reveleer's "33% RAF improvement" are unfalsifiable. A public "How we measure accuracy" page with an evaluation harness description would be unique in the category.
2. **A living regulatory reference (not SEO blogs).** RAAPID has volume, ForeSee has breadth, Datavant has staleness — no one has a dated, changelog-maintained RADV/v28/Rate-Announcement reference hub. The 2025–26 news cycle makes this urgent: CMS announced (May 21, 2025) annual RADV audits of all ~550 eligible MA contracts, 35→200 records sampled, coder workforce 40→2,000, and clearing the PY2018–2024 backlog by early 2026 (https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-rolls-out-aggressive-strategy-enhance-accelerate-medicare-advantage-audits; analysis: https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/05/cms-announces-significant-changes-to-radv-auditing-efforts-considerations-and-next-steps). V28 is 100% phased in for PY2026 (https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-medicare-advantage-part-d-rate-announcement).
3. **Suspect-logic transparency.** Only the open-source Tuva Project publishes actual suspecting logic (https://thetuvaproject.com/data-marts/hcc-suspecting). No commercial vendor documents what feeds their suspects (labs, meds, prior claims, HIE) or per-source confidence tiers. Publishing suspect taxonomy + evidence rules is a defensible trust move competitors' legal teams will resist matching.
4. **Pricing models.** Universal omission. Even publishing the *pricing model* (PMPM vs. per-chart vs. per-gap-closed, what drives cost) without dollar figures would be unmatched.
5. **A real trust center.** Everyone shows badges; no one runs a self-serve trust portal (SafeBase/Vanta-style) with SOC 2 access, subprocessor lists, pen-test summaries, BAA terms, AI-governance policy.
6. **Public integration documentation.** No vendor publishes EHR integration architecture (Epic Connection Hub listing details, CDS Hooks flows, FHIR resources used, athenahealth API usage). athenahealth itself publishes a Risk Adjustment API reference (https://docs.athenahealth.com/api/api-ref/risk-adjustment) — vendors don't reciprocate.
7. **Interactive product demo.** Zero interactive demos in the set; only screenshots/GIFs. A Navattic/Storylane-style walkthrough would be category-first.
8. **Named, dated, methodology-backed case studies.** Navina's AAFP study is the only independent evidence — and it's gated. Publishing ungated studies with named customers, evaluation windows, and denominators is open ground.
9. **Third-party verifiability surfaces.** Buyers are turning to RFP.wiki-style intermediaries for uptime/SLA/reliability facts vendors won't publish (https://www.rfp.wiki/specialty-industries/healthcare-life-sciences/healthcare/healthcare-risk-adjustment-software/reveleer) — publish those facts first-party.

## Part 2 — Best-in-Class "Publish the Rules" Exemplars

### 1. Vanta — compliance framework collections
- **What:** Framework hub "collections" per regulation — SOC 2, ISO 27001, ISO 42001, HIPAA, GDPR, HITRUST, CMMC, FedRAMP (https://www.vanta.com/resources, e.g., /collection/soc-2) — plus downloadable checklists and templates (`/resources/hipaa-compliance-checklist-guide`, `/resources/iso-27001-documentation-template-free-download`), Vanta Academy courses, and community.
- **Structure:** One hub page per framework aggregating guides, checklists, videos; consistent URL taxonomy.
- **Copy for Martlet:** One hub per regulatory object — `/regulations/radv`, `/regulations/cms-hcc-v28`, `/regulations/rate-announcement-2027`, `/regulations/oig-audits` — each with a checklist artifact (e.g., "RADV audit readiness checklist," ungated).

### 2. Drata — GRC Central / "Learn with Drata"
- **What:** 30+ curated collections with visible article counts (SOC 2: 41, HIPAA: 35, Risk Management: 51) under `/learn/[topic-slug]` (https://www.drata.com/grc-central) — including fast-moving topics (EU AI Act, AI Agent Governance, Compliance as Code).
- **Structure:** Collection = topic + count + curated sequence; signals corpus depth at a glance.
- **Copy for Martlet:** A "Learn risk adjustment" library with visible counts and collections: V28 (n articles), RADV (n), Prospective workflows (n), ACO/REACH (n). Add an emerging-topic collection ("AI governance in risk adjustment") competitors won't touch.

### 3. Stripe — guides as reference infrastructure
- **What:** https://stripe.com/guides — country/tax/regulation explainers ("Introduction to US sales tax and economic nexus," "VAT registration in Europe," PCI compliance primer, "machine learning for fraud" primer — a vendor explaining its own algorithms' domain), plus Atlas startup guides.
- **Structure:** Consistent template, keyword-rich titles matching search intent, spanning awareness→consideration.
- **Copy for Martlet:** State-by-state / program-by-program guides ("Risk adjustment in ACO REACH vs. MSSP vs. MA," "Medicaid CDPS+Rx vs. CMS-HCC," "RxHCC explained") and a "primer on NLP for HCC extraction" — Stripe-style: explain the domain mechanics honestly, product mentioned last.

### 4. Plaid — regulatory readiness + developer transparency
- **What:** Topic hubs (payments, fraud, compliance, open finance) with pieces like "ACH Payments 101" and a "Readiness Check for Nacha Fraud Monitoring Rule Changes" (https://plaid.com/resources/); fully open API docs, GitHub quickstarts; Harris Poll co-branded research; consumer trust/safety section.
- **Structure:** Educator-first hubs + open developer surface + research reports.
- **Copy for Martlet:** "Readiness check" format for each CMS rule change (RADV expansion readiness check, V28 completion readiness check); open integration docs; an annual co-branded "State of Risk Adjustment" survey report.

### 5. LaunchDarkly — glossary + comparison content with attribution
- **What:** Open docs + Academy, glossary-style explainers, honest comparison content ("Feature flags vs. feature branching"), author-attributed posts, third-party ROI validation (Forrester TEI) (https://launchdarkly.com/blog/).
- **Structure:** Category-tagged blog, named authors with photos, docs home + quickstarts public.
- **Copy for Martlet:** Named-author expert content (coders, clinicians on staff — bylines build E-E-A-T in a category of anonymous marketing); honest comparison pages ("prospective vs. retrospective," "in-EHR vs. sidecar app"); commission or cite independent ROI validation.

### 6. IAPP / multistate.ai / Orrick — the regulation tracker pattern
- **What:** IAPP's US State AI Governance Legislation Tracker (https://iapp.org/resources/article/us-state-ai-governance-legislation-tracker) — a maintained table with scope columns, obligation categories, and a public correction channel (research@iapp.org); parallel trackers by law firms (https://ai-law-center.orrick.com/us-ai-law-tracker-see-all-states/, https://www.steptoe.com/en/ai-legislative-tracker.html) and multistate.ai (https://www.multistate.ai/artificial-intelligence-ai-legislation). These become the canonical, perpetually-cited URLs in their field.
- **Copy for Martlet:** The single highest-leverage asset: a **maintained CMS Risk Adjustment Tracker** — RADV audit activity by payment year, Rate Announcement/Advance Notice milestones, model version phase-ins (MA, ACO REACH, RxHCC, Medicaid), submission deadlines — as a dated, changelogged table with a corrections email. Nothing like it exists from any vendor.

### 7. (Bonus, in-category) The Tuva Project — open-source suspecting logic
- **What:** Publishes actual HCC suspecting logic with data sources and SQL on GitHub — e.g., CKD via eGFR labs, depression via meds + PHQ-9, diabetes via CKD comorbidity, morbid obesity via vitals + comorbidities (https://thetuvaproject.com/data-marts/hcc-suspecting).
- **Copy for Martlet:** Proof that suspect-logic transparency is publishable. Martlet can publish its suspect taxonomy and evidence rules (even without full code) and be the only commercial vendor to do so.

## Part 3 — Prospective Risk Adjustment Buyer Intelligence

### 3.1 What "prospective" buyers are actually buying
Prospective risk adjustment = identifying, confirming, and documenting conditions **at or before the point of care**, pairing pre-visit planning with in-visit decision support, then validating before claim submission — vs. retrospective chart mining after the fact (https://www.raapidinc.com/blogs/prospective-risk-adjustment/, https://www.navina.ai/articles/understanding-retrospective-vs-prospective-risk-adjustment, https://www.vbcriskanalytics.com/blogs/prospective-vs-retrospective-risk-adjustment). The 2025–26 RADV expansion strengthens the pitch: point-of-care capture with clinical evidence is inherently more audit-defensible than retrospective addition (https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-rolls-out-aggressive-strategy-enhance-accelerate-medicare-advantage-audits).

### 3.2 Pre-visit planning workflows (what buyers search)
- Analytics generate pre-visit condition lists from prior claims, pharmacy data, and clinical patterns; care teams review during huddles; clinicians confirm at the encounter (https://www.aapc.com/blog/90855-the-power-and-profitability-of-pre-visit-risk-adjustment-reviews/ — AAPC frames pre-visit RA review as both a revenue and compliance function).
- Buyer evaluation criteria coalescing in 2026 buyer's guides: evidence-backed suspecting with MEAT validation, combined retro+prospective coverage, coder productivity, CMS model-version accuracy and submission quality (https://www.raapidinc.com/blogs/risk-adjustment-software/, https://arcadia.io/resources/risk-adjustment-software).
- Search terms buyers use: "pre-visit planning HCC," "point-of-care risk adjustment," "HCC recapture workflow," "annual wellness visit HCC," "suspecting engine."

### 3.3 Suspecting methodology — what data feeds suspects
Documented suspect classes (buyers increasingly ask vendors to break these out; almost none do publicly):
- **Prior-year gaps (recapture):** previously coded HCCs not yet documented this year — highest confidence tier.
- **Pharmacy-inferred:** active fills without diagnosis (metformin/insulin → diabetes). See Reveleer's RxHCC suspecting piece (https://www.reveleer.com/resource/rxhcc-suspecting).
- **Lab-inferred:** HbA1c ≥6.5 without diabetes code; eGFR <30 without CKD stage 4.
- **HIE/ADT-derived:** hospital utilization signals, with coverage-completeness caveats.
- **Statistical/clinical-algorithm suspects:** model-predicted conditions across combined sources — the lowest-trust tier and the one buyers most want explained.
Sources: https://thetuvaproject.com/data-marts/hcc-suspecting, https://www.vbcriskanalytics.com/blogs/suspect-conditions-risk-adjustment, https://www.doctustech.com/blog/turning-aggregated-and-filtered-data-into-accurate-hcc-coding-with-ai/, https://www.invene.com/blog/risk-adjustment-coding.
**Content implication:** a public "anatomy of a suspect" page — data source, rule, confidence tier, evidence shown to the clinician — directly answers the #1 unanswered buyer question.

### 3.4 EHR integration patterns buyers evaluate
- **Epic:** CDS Hooks (HL7 standard; hook fires on workflow event, prefetch delivers FHIR resources, card returned in-workflow) is Epic-supported; vendors surface suspects via BPA (Best Practice Advisory) in Epic's HCC risk adjustment workflow, with suspected diagnoses written to the problem list for clinician reconciliation (https://open.epic.com/Interface/FHIR, https://open.epic.com/Clinical/Other, https://arcadia.io/risk-suspecting-software-with-epic, https://medblocks.com/blog/hl7-cds-hooks-a-practical-guide, https://ecqi.healthit.gov/tool/cds-hooks). Buyers ask: Connection Hub/Showroom listing? BPA vs. sidebar (SMART on FHIR) vs. problem-list write-back? Prefetch or on-demand FHIR calls?
- **athenahealth:** Marketplace/athenaConnect listings (300+ partners; Navina listed: https://athenaconnect.athenahealth.com/marketplace/product/navina) and — notably — athenahealth publishes a dedicated public **Risk Adjustment API reference** (https://docs.athenahealth.com/api/api-ref/risk-adjustment) plus a developer portal (https://www.athenahealth.com/developer-portal). Co-marketing pattern: athenahealth+Navina webinars with Privia (https://www.navina.ai/webinars/navina-and-athenahealth-webinar-privia-health-empowers-providers-al).
- **Buyer diligence artifacts:** integration architecture diagram, data elements read/written, refresh latency, downtime behavior, and marketplace listing status. No RA vendor publishes these today (Part 1).

### 3.5 Clinician adoption metrics buyers ask for
- Alert-science literature: the standard CDS metrics are **alert quantity, override rate, and acceptance rate**; excessive low-value alerts drive fatigue and distrust (systematic review: https://academic.oup.com/jamia/advance-article/doi/10.1093/jamia/ocag064/8684938; qualitative GP study: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11845892/; JMIR 2026: https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e78676). Buyers translate this into vendor questions: suspect acceptance rate, dismiss-with-reason rate, alerts per encounter, time-in-tool.
- The benchmark evidence buyers cite: **AAFP Innovation Lab study of Navina** — 61% reduction in chart-prep time (14.1 → 5.5 min), 25% more diagnoses captured, 37% RAF increase, 27% preparedness increase, ~991 encounters, 10 physicians/3 practices; burnout −23% (https://www.aafp.org/practice-operations/administrative-simplification/chart-review/technologies-chart-review-burden, https://www.navina.ai/news/aafp-study-navina-ai-reduces-burnout, https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/ai-improves-physician-readiness-for-patients-while-cutting-time-spent-on-charts, https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/navina-and-aafp-extend-study-of-ai-for-clinical-review-after-innovation-lab-finds-the-ai-platform-simultaneously-reduces-burdens-and-improves-value-based-outcomes-301695141.html). Adoption proof-point format: "90% of doctors used it within the first week" (Navina/Jefferson City Medical Group).
- **AAFP/physician-burden angle:** AAFP maintains a chart-review-burden program page (https://www.aafp.org/family-physician/practice-and-career/administrative-simplification/chart-review/technologies-chart-review-burden.html) — the physician-professional-society framing ("reduce administrative burden") is the door-opener for provider-side sales, not RAF lift.

### 3.6 CDI query reduction
- Physician query response benchmarks: 65–85% typical; <60% signals systemic breakdown (ACDIS data via https://medcodexhealth.com/blog/physician-query-best-practices-cdi-response-strategies); one analysis claims ~60% of CDI queries go unanswered (https://www.homrcm.com/blogs/the-provider-query-response-crisis-why-60-of-cdi-queries-go-unanswered).
- Moving clarification to the point of care raises response rates and cuts retrospective query volume; CDI is shifting from retrospective review to concurrent/prospective AI-flagged documentation opportunities (https://www.raapidinc.com/blogs/outpatient-cdi/, https://patientnotes.ai/resources/cdi-healthcare, AHIMA prospective CDI practice brief: https://www.ahima.org/media/b0jpm2kg/prospective-clinical-documentation-integrity-cdi-reviews-and-query_alert-practice-best-standards_axs.pdf).
- **Buyer metric to publish against:** "queries avoided per 1,000 encounters" / reduction in retrospective query rate — a quantifiable ROI line no vendor currently claims with methodology.

### 3.7 ACOs / MSSP / ACO REACH vs. MA plans as prospective-coding buyers
Key structural differences that change the sales motion:
- **Risk-score caps:** MSSP and REACH cap risk-score growth at **+3%** vs. benchmark-year baseline; REACH adds a retrospective **coding intensity factor** (−1% on faster-than-average individual risk-score growth). MA has no cap — payments move with risk scores (normalized) (https://paragoninstitute.org/medicare/two-paths-for-medicares-future-medicare-advantage-and-accountable-care-organizations/, https://carejourney.com/risk-scoring-in-aco-reach-model/, https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/reactions-py2024-aco-reach-methodology-changes).
- **Consequence:** ACOs buy prospective coding for **accuracy and recapture completeness within the cap** (avoiding under-coding that erodes benchmarks, and demonstrating true acuity), not unlimited RAF lift. Messaging that leads with "RAF lift" reads as compliance risk to an ACO; "complete, defensible capture" wins. Health Affairs documents that ACO risk-adjustment design deters coding increases (https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2018.05407).
- **V28 convergence:** PY2026 fully transitions Standard/New Entrant REACH ACOs to V28, aligning with MA and "raising the bar for documentation accuracy and prospective coding" (https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/aco-reach-py2026-what-is-changing, https://www.pearlhealth.com/blog/aco-reach-2026); MA is 100% V28 in PY2026 (https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-medicare-advantage-part-d-rate-announcement; model mechanics: 9,797→7,770 payment ICD-10 codes, 86→115 HCCs, ~3.12% average risk-score reduction — https://www.rafscorecalculator.com/hcc-v28-guide).
- **Program choice content opportunity:** MSSP vs. REACH comparison content is actively consumed by these buyers (https://www.medbridge.com/blog/mssp-vs-aco-reach) — a vendor-neutral "risk adjustment rules by program" matrix (MA / MSSP / REACH / Medicaid / ACA) would rank and convert.

### 3.8 Prospective-product page must-haves (synthesis)
1. Suspect taxonomy with data sources and confidence tiers, with evidence-display screenshots.
2. Named EHR integration architecture: Epic CDS Hooks/BPA flow diagram, athenahealth API usage, FHIR resources touched, marketplace listing badges.
