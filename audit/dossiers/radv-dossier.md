## 1. Legal basis and history

### Statutory and regulatory authority
- Medicare Advantage payments are risk-adjusted under Social Security Act § 1853(a)(1)(C), which requires CMS to adjust capitation payments for the health status of enrollees. CMS's authority to collect and validate risk adjustment data, and to audit MA organizations, derives from SSA §§ 1853(a)(3) and 1857(d) (the latter requiring annual audits of the financial records of at least one-third of MA organizations). MAOs must also report and return identified overpayments under SSA § 1128J(d), implemented at 42 CFR § 422.326.
  - Sources: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-G/section-422.310 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/422.311 ; https://www.cms.gov/files/document/payment-year-2020-ma-radv-audit-methods-instructions.pdf
- The RADV framework is codified at 42 CFR § 422.310 (risk adjustment data validation) and § 422.311 (RADV audit dispute and appeal processes).
  - Sources: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-G/section-422.311
- Record retention: under 42 CFR § 422.504(d), MAOs (and, via § 422.504(i), their contracted providers) must retain records for 10 years, and the federal government retains the right to audit an MAO through 10 years from the end of the final contract period — including terminated contracts (42 CFR § 422.529(d)).
  - Source: CMS RADV Questions & Answers, updated 05/29/2026 — https://www.cms.gov/files/document/radv-questions-answers.pdf

### Historical audit volumes and recoveries
- CMS historically audited only ~60 MA contracts per payment year (out of roughly 550 eligible contracts), sampling 201 enrollees per contract in early audits and 35 enrollees per contract in the PY2018 cycle.
  - Sources: https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-rolls-out-aggressive-strategy-enhance-accelerate-medicare-advantage-audits ; https://www.cms.gov/files/document/payment-year-2018-ma-radv-audit-methods-instructions.pdf
- Per CMS (May 2025): "The last significant recovery of MA overpayments occurred following the audit of payment year (PY) 2007." CMS's completed audits for PYs 2011–2013 found **5% to 8% overpayment rates**.
  - Source: https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-rolls-out-aggressive-strategy-enhance-accelerate-medicare-advantage-audits
- CMS has been clearing its backlog of legacy audit results: PY2011 audit reports were issued to 30 MA contracts in January 2025; PY2012 and PY2013 payment error calculation (PEC) methodologies and results were published May 13, 2025; PY2014 and PY2015 PEC methodologies and audit results were published March 4, 2026 (contract lists with identified overpayments are downloadable from CMS).
  - Source: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/monitoring-programs/medicare-advantage-risk-adjustment-data-validation-radv-program/radv-announcements

### The February 2023 RADV Final Rule (88 Fed. Reg. 6643; CMS-4185-F2)
Published February 1, 2023 (effective April 3, 2023), the rule:
- **Codified extrapolation of audit findings beginning with PY2018.** For PYs 2011–2017, CMS limited recoveries to non-extrapolated, enrollee-level overpayments identified in CMS RADV and HHS-OIG audits.
- **Eliminated the fee-for-service (FFS) adjuster.** CMS concluded the actuarial-equivalence requirement of the SSA applies to how CMS calculates risk-adjusted payments, not to MAOs' obligation to return improper payments — so no offset for FFS documentation error rates would be applied to audit recoveries.
- CMS projected **$4.7 billion in recoveries over 2023–2032** from extrapolated and non-extrapolated RADV recoveries; industry summaries of the rule's impact analysis cite projected annual recoveries of roughly $479 million once extrapolated collections began (net ~$428 million after ~$51 million annual program cost).
- Sources (primary): https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/02/01/2023-01942/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-policy-and-technical-changes-to-the-medicare-advantage-medicare ; https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicare-advantage-risk-adjustment-data-validation-final-rule-cms-4185-f2-fact-sheet
- Sources (secondary): https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2023/02/cmss-final-radv-rule-announces-no-extrapolation-methodology-no-ffs-adjuster ; https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2023/02/extrapolation-has-arrived-cms-finalizes-medicare-advantage-risk-adjustment-rule ; https://www.reedsmith.com/en/perspectives/2023/02/radv-final-rule-and-its-implications-for-plans ; https://www.csicompanies.com/insights/are-you-ready-for-the-financial-impact-of-a-radv-audit

## 2. The May 2025 expansion and everything since

### CMS press release, May 21, 2025 — "CMS Rolls Out Aggressive Strategy to Enhance and Accelerate Medicare Advantage Audits"
Key commitments (all from the primary source: https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-rolls-out-aggressive-strategy-enhance-accelerate-medicare-advantage-audits):
- **Audit all eligible MA contracts every payment year** in all newly initiated audits (~550 contracts), up from ~60 per year.
- **Complete the backlog of PY2018–PY2024 audits by early 2026** (a target that subsequently slipped; see the published audit schedule below).
- **Workforce scale-up: from 40 to ~2,000 certified medical coders by September 1, 2025**, to manually verify flagged diagnoses.
- **Enhanced technology:** "CMS will deploy advanced systems to efficiently review medical records and flag unsupported diagnoses" (AI-enabled review with human coder verification of flagged diagnoses).
- **Larger samples:** from 35 records per plan per year to **between 35 and 200 records per plan per year** based on plan size — "to help ensure CMS's audit findings are more reliable and can be appropriately extrapolated as allowed under the RADV final rule."
- Context cited by CMS: federal estimates that MA plans "may overbill the government by approximately **$17 billion annually**"; MedPAC's estimate "could be as high as **$43 billion per year**." CMS also said it would collaborate with HHS-OIG to recover uncollected overpayments identified in past audits.
- CMS Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz: "We are committed to crushing fraud, waste and abuse across all federal healthcare programs."

### PY2020 audit specifics (current cycle, initiated March 20, 2026)
From the PY2020 MA RADV Audit Methods & Instructions (primary source: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/payment-year-2020-ma-radv-audit-methods-instructions.pdf):

- **Sampling:** contracts grouped into **four strata by sampling-frame size — sample sizes 200 (top 10 contracts), 100, 50, and 35** (top/middle/bottom thirds of remaining eligible contracts); simple random sample without replacement. ## 3. Extrapolation: methodology and the litigation that paused it

### How CMS's extrapolation methodology works
From the PY2018 and PY2020 Audit Methods & Instructions (primary: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/payment-year-2020-ma-radv-audit-methods-instructions.pdf ; https://www.cms.gov/files/document/payment-year-2018-ma-radv-audit-methods-instructions.pdf):
1. **Enrollee-level payment error.** For each sampled enrollee, CMS re-runs the payment model using only RADV-validated diagnoses to produce a "RADV risk score," recomputes monthly payments against the county rate, and subtracts from what was originally paid → "RADV enrollee total payment error" (can be positive, $0, or negative).
2. **Average risk-score change (ΔR̄):** mean of each sampled enrollee's risk-score change (original risk score minus RADV risk score) across the n sampled enrollees.
3. **Variance and confidence interval:** CMS computes the sample variance, applies a finite-population correction ((N−n)/N), and computes the **lower bound of the 90% confidence interval**: δ = ΔR̄ − 1.645·√VAR(ΔR̄).
4. **Extrapolated overpayment:** ΔR̄ × (sum of county rates for **all N enrollees in the sampling frame**). Extrapolation applies **only if the 90% CI lower bound is greater than zero** (and only if it exceeds the sum of sampled-enrollee errors); otherwise CMS collects sample-level errors only. So the recovery uses the **point estimate**, with the CI lower bound acting as a statistical gate.
5. **Guardrails:** no extrapolation if the sampling frame has fewer than 30 enrollees (CMS audits the whole frame instead) or if granted exceptions drop the sample below 30. "Additional HCCs" found in records can offset overpayments (except "spontaneous" additional HCCs outside the audited hierarchy), but an audit can never generate additional payments to the plan; net underpayments are capped at $0.
- Accessible secondary explanation: https://www.wakely.com/blog/medicare-advantage-radv-extrapolation-understanding-the-framework/ ("extrapolation occurs only when the lower bound of the confidence interval remains positive"; "two audits with similar findings may result in different extrapolation outcomes" depending on error distribution).

### Humana v. Becerra — the September 2025 ruling
- **Case:** *Humana Inc. and Humana Benefit Plan of Texas, Inc. v. Becerra* (now captioned v. Kennedy), No. 4:23-cv-00909-O (N.D. Tex.), filed September 1, 2023; reported at 801 F. Supp. 3d 624 (N.D. Tex. 2025) (citation used by CMS itself in the PY2020 Audit Methods & Instructions).
- **Holding (September 25, 2025):** Chief Judge Reed O'Connor granted Humana summary judgment, **vacated the 2023 RADV Final Rule in its entirety and remanded to CMS**, finding the rule procedurally invalid under the APA: CMS's final-rule rationale for abandoning the FFS adjuster was **not a "logical outgrowth"** of the 2018 proposed rule, depriving commenters of fair notice.
  - Sources (primary): https://litigationtracker.law.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Humana-Inc._2025.09.25_ORDER-ON-MOTION-FOR-SUMMARY-JUDGMENT.pdf ; docket: https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/67755069/humana-inc-v-becerra/
  - Sources (secondary): https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/court-vacates-cmss-2023-final-rule-on-radv-audits ; https://www.groom.com/resources/court-rules-that-cms-cannot-extrapolate-medicare-advantage-risk-adjustment-audit-results/ ; https://www.healthlawadvisor.com/texas-court-strikes-down-cmss-radv-rule-cmss-treatment-of-actuarial-equivalence-and-the-ffs-adjustor-does-matter-after-all ; https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/federal-court-vacates-2023-rule-cms-radv-audits
- **Effect:** with the rule vacated, CMS cannot currently make extrapolated RADV recoveries. Audits themselves continue (sampling/record review are unaffected), but collections are limited to enrollee-level overpayments unless CMS prevails on appeal or re-promulgates a rule.

### Appeal status (as of mid-2026)
- CMS/HHS filed a **notice of appeal on November 21, 2025**; the appeal is pending before the **U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, No. 25-11293**. The government filed its **opening brief on March 21, 2026**. No oral argument date or decision had been reported as of mid-July 2026; district-court status is "appeal filed."
  - Sources: https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/cms-appeals-humana-v-becerra ; https://litigationtracker.law.georgetown.edu/litigation/humana-inc-et-al-v-becerra-et-al/ ; opening brief: https://litigationtracker.law.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Humana_2026.03.21_DEFENDANTS-OPENING-BRIEF.pdf ; https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/trump-administration-cms-appeal-medicare-advantage-audit-ruling-radv/806397/
- **CMS's position pending appeal** (stated in the PY2020/PY2021 Audit Methods & Instructions and the January 2026 memo): comply with the vacatur, collect sampled-enrollee overpayments now, design audits to support extrapolation, and reserve the right to extrapolate later "if legally permissible."
  - Sources: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/payment-year-2020-ma-radv-audit-methods-instructions.pdf ; https://www.risehealth.org/insights-articles/article/cms-update-the-latest-on-fast-track-medicare-advantage-radv-audits/

## 4. The audit lifecycle from the health plan's seat

### Step-by-step (per CMS's PY2020 Audit Methods & Instructions and RADV Q&A)
1. **Selection & notification.** CMS selects contracts (risk-based selection using advanced data analytics plus prior CMS/OIG audit results, per the PY2018 Q&A; all eligible contracts under the 2025+ policy). Notice arrives via **HPMS to the contract's designated points of contact**, generally **a few weeks before the record-submission window opens**, and includes timelines, submission instructions, and how payment errors will be calculated.
2. **Enrollee sample & data package.** CMS posts the **Enrollee Data List (EDL)** in its secure Centralized Data Abstraction Tool (**CDAT**): sampled enrollees, the audited HCCs and underlying diagnosis codes, plus pre-populated Medical Record Coversheets and CMS-generated attestation forms.
3. **Medical record retrieval & internal pre-review (the five-month window).** The plan retrieves records from providers and pre-reviews them. **Cap: up to 2× the number of audited HCCs in medical records per sampled enrollee** (e.g., 3 audited HCCs → max 6 records); **one valid record is sufficient to validate an HCC** ("one best medical record" principle). A record = documentation of a **single face-to-face encounter** (single date of service for physician/outpatient; single admission for inpatient). Plans may submit records from any acceptable provider and any encounter date in the data-collection year — even encounters never previously submitted for payment. **Plans may not amend or ask providers to amend records at audit time.**
4. **Submission via CDAT.** Each record needs its own coversheet; each file contains one record plus (if needed) one completed CMS-generated attestation. CMS runs an **intake process** (correct enrollee, matching names/DOB, completeness, PHI checks) and posts an **Intake Feedback Report**; invalid submissions can be replaced while the window is open. **PY2020 window: April 13 – August 28, 2026.** Hardship exceptions (natural disasters, law-enforcement actions, etc.) may be requested for a contract, enrollee, or HCC — PY2020 deadline September 11, 2026. A change of ownership does not excuse an audit.
5. **CMS coder review.** Certified medical record coders abstract ICD codes per **ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines and AHA Coding Clinic**. **Up to three rounds of review:** initial abstraction on all valid records; a second review of records that failed to substantiate the audited HCC; a third, tie-breaking review where the first two disagree.
6. **Findings.** CMS issues an **Audit Report Package** in CDAT: per-enrollee, per-HCC outcomes — **Confirmed, Confirmed Higher, Discrepant, Discrepant Lower, or Administrative Exception** — plus payment error calculations and collection instructions.
7. **Appeals (42 CFR § 422.311(c)).** Three administrative levels: **(1) Reconsideration** — written request through CDAT **within 60 days** of the audit report; plans may appeal **medical record review determinations and/or payment error calculations** (only "Discrepant"/"Discrepant Lower" findings are appealable; additional-HCC credit decisions are not appealable). **(2) Hearing** — request to the CMS Hearing Officer within **60 days** of the reconsideration decision. **(3) CMS Administrator review.** Appeals deemed ineligible at reconsideration cannot proceed to hearing.
- Sources (all primary): https://www.cms.gov/files/document/payment-year-2020-ma-radv-audit-methods-instructions.pdf ; https://www.cms.gov/files/document/radv-questions-answers.pdf ; https://www.cms.gov/files/document/ma-radv-reconsiderations-guidance-01-16-2025-pdf.pdf ; https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-G/section-422.311 ; https://www.cms.gov/files/document/office-hearings-medicare-advantage-risk-adjustment-data-validation-level-ii-appeal-procedures.pdf ; https://www.cms.gov/medicare/regulations-guidance/cms-hearing-officer/medicare-advantage-risk-adjustment-data-validation-appeals

### Documentation standards CMS coders apply
From CMS's Contract-Level RADV Medical Record Reviewer Guidance (primary: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/medical-record-reviewer-guidance-january-2020.pdf):
- **Minimum validity requirements:** (1) correct beneficiary; (2) **acceptable risk-adjustment provider type, source, and physician specialty for a face-to-face encounter**; (3) **dates of service within the data-collection year** (e.g., PY2020 payments validate against DOS Jan 1–Dec 31, 2019); (4) **valid signature and credentials**; (5) coding consistent with ICD Official Guidelines and AHA Coding Clinic in effect at the encounter date.
- **Signatures:** handwritten or qualifying electronic signatures only; **stamped signatures are not acceptable**. Electronic signatures need authentication language ("Electronically signed by," "Authenticated by," etc.) plus name and credentials; e-signatures dated **more than 180 days after the encounter** are case-by-case, and signatures obtained in response to the RADV request are not acceptable. Transcribed/dictated reports must be signed — a typed name with transcriptionist ID is insufficient. For **inpatient records, a typed signature alone is not acceptable**; unsigned portions of an inpatient record are excluded from coding (but a few unsigned notes don't invalidate the whole record).
- **Attestations:** a missing/illegible signature or credential on **physician/outpatient records only** can be cured with the **CMS-generated attestation form** (no other attestation format accepted), signed and dated by the rendering physician/practitioner, for a **single date of service** matching the record. Attestations **cannot validate dates or diagnoses** — only authenticate the record. Invalid-attestation reasons include: altered, incomplete, non-CMS form, DOS mismatch, unacceptable credentials, or signed by someone other than the practitioner.
- **Provider types:** records must come from inpatient hospital, outpatient hospital, or an **acceptable physician specialty** on CMS's risk-adjustment list (guidance appendices list acceptable specialty types); credentials must appear on the record or stationery.
- **Unacceptable sources** include: standalone diagnosis/ICD code lists without narrative, claim forms (usable to verify dates/credentials but never for coding), superbills, durable medical equipment records, ambulance records, hospice-unit records, home health agency documentation/CMS-485 forms without a face-to-face physician visit, intermediate care facilities, lab results alone, and patient-reported narratives.
- **Problem lists** are evaluated case-by-case for chronicity and consistency with the encounter documentation; conditions merely listed may not support an HCC without supporting evidence elsewhere in the record (history, medications, assessment).
- The overarching pre-2020 principle CMS repeats: supporting records must be "clear and unambiguous," and the coder validates **codes against written documentation**, not claims data.

### The most common failure patterns (from OIG's December 2023 high-risk-code toolkit and audit series)
- **Acute conditions coded as current when only "history of" was supported** — acute stroke and acute MI reported on a physician/office claim with **no corresponding inpatient hospital claim**; OIG found **>90% of such stroke and heart-attack codes unsupported**.
- **Cancers (lung, breast, colon, prostate) coded as active** with no treatment or active management in the service year (should have been history-of codes, which don't risk-adjust).
- **Embolism** coded without anticoagulant management; **sepsis** without an inpatient claim; **major depressive disorder** without documented severity/treatment; **vascular claudication** and **pressure ulcer** groups; **potentially mis-keyed codes**.
- Overall, OIG found **~70% of audited high-risk diagnosis codes were not supported** by the medical record (as of Nov 2023).
- Sources: https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2023/toolkit-to-help-decrease-improper-payments-in-medicare-advantage-through-the-identification-of-high-risk-diagnosis-codes/ ; https://fcablog.sidley.com/2024/01/02/hhs-oig-releases-medicare-advantage-toolkit-to-guide-internal-auditing/ ; https://www.risehealth.org/insights-articles/article/oig-s-new-toolkit-aims-to-cut-improper-payments-in-medicare-advantage/
- In RADV audits specifically, HCCs also commonly fail on **technical/documentation grounds** rather than clinical ones: unsigned or improperly signed records, missing credentials, unacceptable provider type or source document, DOS outside the collection year, no valid face-to-face encounter, and problem-list-only documentation (see §4 documentation standards; source: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/medical-record-reviewer-guidance-january-2020.pdf ).

### Program-level overpayment estimates
- **OIG (Oct 2024, OEI-03-23-00380):** diagnoses reported **only** on health risk assessments and HRA-linked chart reviews — with no other 2022 encounter — generated an estimated **$7.5 billion in 2023 MA risk-adjusted payments**. In-home HRAs and linked chart reviews drove **$4.2B** of it; **20 MA companies drove 80%**; UnitedHealth Group and Humana together accounted for **>$5.4B**; 13 conditions drove 75% ($5.6B).

## 6. Financial exposure math: sample → contract-level recovery

**Mechanics** (per CMS's published methodology, §3 above): the audit's average per-enrollee risk-score error (ΔR̄) is multiplied by the **sum of county rates for every enrollee in the contract's sampling frame** — not just the sample. A 200-enrollee sample therefore prices the error for the tens or hundreds of thousands of enrollees behind it, provided the 90% CI lower bound of the risk-score error exceeds zero.
- Source: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/payment-year-2020-ma-radv-audit-methods-instructions.pdf (§9.2)

**Illustration (Martlet's own math, methodology per CMS):** take a contract with a 50,000-enrollee sampling frame and an average annual county-rate payment base of ~$10,000 per enrollee. If the audited 200-enrollee sample shows an average validated risk-score drop of 0.03 (about 3% of a 1.00 risk score), the extrapolated recovery ≈ 0.03 × $10,000 × 50,000 = **$15 million** — versus roughly **$60,000** if recovery were limited to the 200 sampled enrollees. That ~250× multiplier (frame size ÷ sample size) is the entire economic stake of the extrapolation litigation.

**Published magnitude estimates:**
- CMS (2023 final rule): **$4.7B in expected recoveries 2023–2032**; industry summaries of the rule's impact analysis put expected annual recoveries at **~$479.4M/year** once extrapolated collections begin (~$428M net of ~$51M program cost) — versus the historic pattern of low-eight-figure recoveries per audited payment year without extrapolation.
  - Sources: https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicare-advantage-risk-adjustment-data-validation-final-rule-cms-4185-f2-fact-sheet ; https://www.csicompanies.com/insights/are-you-ready-for-the-financial-impact-of-a-radv-audit ; https://www.reedsmith.com/en/perspectives/2023/02/radv-final-rule-and-its-implications-for-plans
- Historic benchmark: CMS's completed PY2011–2013 audits found **5–8% payment error**; MA plans received roughly $455B in 2024, so even low-single-digit extrapolated error rates across ~550 contracts imply multi-billion-dollar annual exposure — the reason MedPAC/CMS cite $17–43B in coding-driven overpayments (see §5).
  - Sources: https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-rolls-out-aggressive-strategy-enhance-accelerate-medicare-advantage-audits
- Wakely's practitioner framing: extrapolated impact "can range from minimal recoveries to substantial repayment obligations," and two audits with similar raw findings can extrapolate differently depending on error variance (the CI-lower-bound gate).
  - Source: https://www.wakely.com/blog/medicare-advantage-radv-extrapolation-understanding-the-framework/
- Note: pending the Fifth Circuit appeal, PY2020+ collections are currently limited to sampled-enrollee overpayments, with extrapolated collection expressly reserved "if legally permissible" (see §§2–3).

## 7. HHS-RADV (ACA / commercial risk adjustment) — brief

For the ACA individual and small-group markets, HHS runs a parallel program — **HHS-RADV** — to validate the risk adjustment data issuers submit through their EDGE servers. Each benefit year, every risk-adjustment-covered issuer (with limited exemptions) hires an **Initial Validation Auditor (IVA)** to re-abstract demographic, enrollment, and health-status data for a random sample of enrollees; HHS's **Second Validation Auditor (SVA)** then re-checks a subsample of the IVA's work for quality assurance. HHS computes issuer-level **HCC failure rates**, groups HCCs into low/medium/high failure-rate tiers, and — when an issuer's failure rate falls outside statistical bounds — adjusts the issuer's plan-average risk score, which flows through to **modified risk-adjustment transfers among all issuers in the state market risk pool** (typically applied to the following benefit year). Unlike MA-RADV, HHS-RADV is zero-sum among issuers rather than a government clawback, and it includes its own error-estimation, discrepancy-reporting, and administrative appeals processes.
- Sources: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/12/01/2020-26338/amendments-to-the-hhs-operated-risk-adjustment-data-validation-hhs-radv-under-the-patient-protection ; https://www.cms.gov/files/document/2019-hhs-risk-adjustment-data-validation-hhs-radv-white-paper.pdf ; https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/document/hhs-risk-adjustment-data-validation-hhs-radv-faq-3
